## Findings (non-VERIFIED)

### 1. AMBIGUOUS — dropped condition on the both-auth-params MAY (extraction lines 30 and 61)

Claim (line 30): "Both auth-params MAY occur in the same challenge"
Claim (line 61, normative table): "MAY | RS | Include both `max_age` and `acr_values` in the same challenge | §3"

Source (§3): "The auth-params max_age and acr_values MAY both occur in the same challenge if the resource server needs to express requirements about both recency and authentication level."

Verdict: AMBIGUOUS (minor). The extraction drops the qualifying condition "if the resource server needs to express requirements about both recency and authentication level" in both places. The permission itself is correctly stated and the omission does not change what an implementer may do, but the table row presents an unconditional MAY where the source ties it to a stated need. Contrast: the adjacent scope-attribute MAY (line 62) correctly retains its condition.

### 2. AMBIGUOUS — metaphor note strengthens the source's claim (extraction line 69)

Claim: "'Authentication level' and 'step up' are explicitly metaphors: no absolute, interoperable hierarchy of authentication methods exists (§2)."

Source (§2): "The terms 'authentication level' and 'step up' are metaphors in this specification. These metaphors do not suggest that there is an absolute hierarchy of authentication methods expressed in interoperable fashion."

Verdict: AMBIGUOUS (minor). The source says the metaphors "do not suggest that there is" such a hierarchy; the extraction converts this into a positive assertion that no such hierarchy "exists". The spec's evident intent (levels emerge only from what an RS will accept) supports the reading, but strictly the RFC declines to assert the hierarchy's nonexistence — it only disclaims implying one.
